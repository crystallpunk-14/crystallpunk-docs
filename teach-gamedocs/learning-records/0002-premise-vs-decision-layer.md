# Misconception: "the decision-log approach means you never write the foundation down"

After lesson 0001 the user (TheShuEd) pushed back: his core gameplay concept (the
protagonist is an "island" city of dozens of role-split players, not one hero) felt
like something the ADR approach told him not to document, which he found strange.

The correction: the foundation *is* documented, once, in the **vision / premise layer**
(~1 paragraph). Lesson 0001 only covered the decision-log layer (record #3 of 3:
vision, pillars, decision log) because it maps onto all four of his stated pains, but
leading with it created the impression that description is banned outright. It is not.
What the approach kills is the multi-page GDD that re-describes every role and system;
the premise stays, compressed.

## The sorting test established

- **Premise (vision layer):** true by definition; change it and it is a different game;
  you are declaring a frame, not weighing options. ("The hero is the island.")
- **Decision (log layer):** you chose X and could plausibly have chosen Y; the record
  carries the foreclosed alternatives. ("The thaumaturgy academy owns city progression"
  — engineers or adventurers could have owned it.)

## Evidence this landed / what it revealed

The user's own D-0001 draft ("academy owns city progression") had "no alternatives
considered" in the rejected-alternatives section, and he reported "strong doubts" while
writing it. Diagnosis given: he recorded a *decision* as if it were *premise*. The
discomfort was the thinking-tool function working — the empty alternatives section is
the record telling him the choice is not actually settled (distributed progression
across factions is a live alternative he has not ruled out).
